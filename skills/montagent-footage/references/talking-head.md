# Talking-head structure

A speaker to camera, cut for a feed or a page: a social clip, a presenter spot, an interview answer. The words carry it, so every layer serves the words.

## The layers, bottom to top

| Layer | What | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Ground | a flat brand colour, a soft still, or the original background | behind a keyed speaker, one flat colour reads cleanest; keep text-coloured grounds out, since the captions must read over it |
| Speaker | the take, keyed or not | eyes 25–35 % down; the speaker never jumps sideways between shots |
| Inserts | picture-in-picture, product clip, still | beside the speaker, never over the face |
| Name bar | who or what is speaking | on early, off before the midpoint |
| Captions | every spoken word | on from the first word to the last |

## Timing

- **The first 1–2 s** decide whether anyone stays: the speaker is already talking at frame 0, with the first caption page up. No logo intro and no fade from black.
- **The name bar** arrives on the first pause or at 1 s, whichever is first, and holds 4–6 s, long enough to read twice.
- **An insert** arrives on the word that names what it shows, and leaves on a pause, never mid-word. One insert on screen at a time.
- **The end:** the last word is followed by 0.3–1 s of hold (the speaker, or an end card of the lockup), with the music back up under it (see "Music under a voice" in SKILL.md), fading out on the last drawn frame.
- **Cuts inside a take** go on a pause of 150 ms or more. A cut mid-word jolts even when the picture matches.

## With the sound off

Most viewers start muted, so the captions are the piece. Check each page reads on its own, with no page shorter than the time it takes to read it; `validate`'s caption findings are the numbers for that. Keep the highlighted word and the accent colour the only accent on screen at that moment: a name bar and a caption both in the accent compete.

## Interviews and two speakers

- A change of speaker is a cut on a pause, or a slide of the lower-third to the new name. Never both in the same frame.
- Captions for two speakers keep one position and one style, with the speaker's name bar showing who is talking. Colour-coding speakers takes an accent away from the spoken-word highlight.
