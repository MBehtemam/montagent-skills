# Creative direction for Montagent motion graphics

Use this reference to turn a brief into a visual concept before generating the project. Keep the requested length, assets and deliverable. Existing brand guidance supplies the palette, fonts and tone; Montagent's craft skill supplies the timing, type sizes, safe areas and restraint.

## Choose a concept

State the message in one sentence, then choose a visual action that makes it concrete. A feature that organizes things might assemble scattered pieces into a row; a before-and-after might replace one state with another; a logo sting might reveal the mark through a shape related to it. An intentional still hold is also a direction.

Write a short direction beside the project: message, visual action, approved copy and assets, palette and type roles. Reuse a direction already present. Ask only for missing choices that materially affect the result; an explicit request for a video authorizes building it without a separate storyboard approval.

## Compose a video frame

Design the frame at its delivery size, then inspect it at the likely viewing size. Use the real screenshot or logo when supplied. If a screenshot's useful detail is too small, crop toward that detail or split the explanation across shots.

Assign each visible layer a job: establish the ground, carry the message, or guide attention. A divider can connect a label to a value; a color panel can separate two states. Add depth only where it clarifies those roles. A sparse logo sting can be complete with a ground and a mark.

Choose a layout from the content: a left-aligned title with a product on the right, a large number with its explanation beneath, or a centered mark for a reveal. Keep essential copy legible against every background it crosses. Read the type and safe-area rules in the craft skill before measuring text.

## Choreograph the beat sheet

Extend the existing beat sheet with a focal subject, motion verb, supporting response and handoff. Describe what the viewer should notice, then choose the matching native recipe.

| Moment | Viewer notices | Native recipe |
|---|---|---|
| Problem becomes clear | A phrase arrives in reading order | Kinetic text |
| Parts become a system | Cards land in a deliberate sequence | Pop and stagger |
| Progress reaches a result | A bar grows toward its label | Grow from an edge |
| One state replaces another | The new panel moves across the frame | Push, slide or wipe |
| Identity resolves | A covered mark becomes visible | Reveal by wipe, then hold |

Use related motion for related objects. Make a supporting label arrive after its subject, and keep the focal move readable before the next starts. Let the handoff express a relationship: a push for adjacent stages, a reveal for discovery, or a cut for an immediate change. Use the existing motion recipes and craft timing rather than importing browser animation APIs.

## Check the concept in the render loop

Inspect the settled state, the busiest overlap and the handoff. Preview each focal move to check reading order and pace. If the scene feels crowded, remove a competing move or split the message before adding effects. Finish through the router's render and verification loop.

For an effect the native format cannot express, load the prerender skill and bake an external asset through its supported route. Keep its source recipe beside the asset; the Montagent project uses ordinary footage or images.
